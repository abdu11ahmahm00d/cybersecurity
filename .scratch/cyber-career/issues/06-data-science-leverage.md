# Does the data-science history convert into an advantage, or is it sunk cost?

Type: research
Status: open
Blocked by: 02

## Question

The owner has one published data-science paper and three in progress, plus production DS skills (Python,
statistics, ML). Determine where that is an asset in security and where it is irrelevant:

1. **AI/ML security** — prompt injection, model and data poisoning, agentic-app pentesting, MLSecOps.
   Is this a real hiring category in 2026, or still mostly conference talks? What do the job ads ask for?
2. **Detection engineering and security analytics** — SIEM rule writing, anomaly detection, threat hunting
   with data. Does an ML background help or does it produce people who cannot read a log?
3. **Security research / academic route** — do the papers help for a master's abroad in security, and does a
   research career in security need a PhD in most markets?
4. The counter-argument: what does the DS background *cost* — e.g. habits that misfire (trusting models over
   systems knowledge), and the expectation gap in interviews.
5. Concrete proof-of-work ideas that turn the DS history into security evidence (a detection-engineering
   repo, an ML-evasion writeup, a published security paper).
