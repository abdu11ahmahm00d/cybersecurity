# Does offensive knowledge imply defensive knowledge? Is defensive "easier"?

Type: research
Status: open
Blocked by: none

## Question

The brief contains a specific causal claim, from a friend doing offensive security: *"What's the point of
learning defensive security if you know offensive security — the defensive one is easier, so you already
know it."* Test it.

1. What do defensive roles actually require that offensive knowledge does not supply — detection
   engineering, log and telemetry analysis (SIEM), incident response process, threat hunting hypotheses,
   identity and hardening at scale, regulatory reporting, forensic evidence handling?
2. What is the direction of skill transfer in practice, and what do practitioners and hiring managers say
   about it? Is "purple team" the actual answer, or a conference slogan?
3. Where is the *market* easier: which of the two has more entry-level seats, and which has a harder gate
   (offensive work often demands proof of skill; defensive work often demands shift tolerance and process)?
4. Name the counter-examples: offensive people who fail at defensive work and vice versa, with reasons.
