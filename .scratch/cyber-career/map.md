# Map — Cybersecurity as final-year major and career

## Destination

A decision the owner can defend out loud — *take cybersecurity as the final-year major and career track, or
don't* — backed by a verified certificate ladder, a verified picture of the Bangladesh job market
(offensive vs defensive, entry-level reality), an 8-book reading list, and a study plan crosswalked to the
university curriculum, all written down in this repo.

## Notes

- **Domain**: cybersecurity education and early-career entry, from a data-science background, based in
  Bangladesh, with going abroad as a parallel option.
- **Owner's standing facts**: final-year undergraduate, data science; one published paper, three in
  progress; no prior security knowledge; strong work ethic, willing to grind certificates; wants to learn
  deeply, from books, not only from videos.
- **Skills every session should consult**: `grilling` (decisions are the owner's), `domain-modeling`
  (vocabulary in [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md) is canonical), `research` (claims get sources),
  `study-agent` (for the teaching sessions themselves).
- **Standing preference**: plan first, execute on request. Artifacts the owner explicitly asked for — the
  reading list, the ladder, the study plan — are in scope for this effort; general "learn everything" is not.
- **Evidence rule**: no claim ships without a source and a date. Vendor marketing counts as evidence of
  what the vendor claims, nothing more.
- **Tracker**: local markdown in this repo (`.scratch/cyber-career/`).

## Not yet specified

- Which role family the owner actually aims at, and whether that choice is made before or after the first
  certificate. Hangs on the market picture and on the owner's appetite for hands-on vs policy work.
- Whether the degree itself (coursework, thesis, CGPA) is the lever, or the certificates are, or both —
  depends on what the university curriculum actually contains.
- How the existing data-science research output converts into a security specialisation (AI/ML security,
  detection engineering, security research) rather than being written off.
- Whether going abroad is a parallel track (master's) or a fallback, and what that does to the ladder order.
- The obfuscated "PT1" the friend recommended, and whether his advice holds once named.
- Budget envelope for exams, labs and books, and how to sequence spend against a student's income.

## Out of scope

- Building a general-purpose cybersecurity curriculum for other people. This map serves one decision.
- Teaching material that belongs to a paid course; the repo links and cites, it does not pirate.
